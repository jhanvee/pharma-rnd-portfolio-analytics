# Illustrative Investment Memo
## Pharmaceutical R&D Portfolio Valuation & Risk Analytics
**Status:** Independent learning project; all inputs and programmes are hypothetical.

### Purpose
Compare five fictional development programmes using a simplified discounted cash-flow framework and Monte Carlo uncertainty analysis. This is a demonstration of analytical workflow, not a clinical or investment recommendation.

### Suggested review questions
1. Which programmes contribute most to expected portfolio value?
2. How sensitive are programme rankings to development delays and peak-sales assumptions?
3. Which programmes show the widest downside distribution?
4. What additional scientific, clinical, commercial, regulatory and market-access evidence would be required before making a funding decision?
5. What are the opportunity costs of funding one programme over another?

### Important limitations
- Success probabilities are illustrative inputs, not empirical stage-transition estimates.
- The model uses a simplified sales ramp and does not explicitly model indication-level epidemiology, patient eligibility, price/reimbursement, competition, patents, taxes, partnering, royalties, or launch costs in detail.
- The simplified rNPV method probability-weights commercial cash flows but treats development spending as certain; this is a teaching approximation, not a complete pharma valuation methodology.
- The Monte Carlo model uses independent distributions and a simplified Bernoulli success/failure outcome; real portfolio risks may be correlated and require stage-specific outcome trees.
- No scientific or clinical diligence has been performed.

### Conclusion
The output should be used to demonstrate how a finance analyst can structure assumptions, calculate value, quantify uncertainty, visualise results and identify questions for decision-makers. No programme should be funded or discontinued based on this model alone.
